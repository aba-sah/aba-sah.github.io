# Bo'ness Foreshore to Blackness via Shore Wood
	The path continues on to rejoin the John Muir Way. At the other end od the harbour is another marker describing more roman artefacts unearthed in the area. The surfaced path goes inward slightly past another boatyard, then emerges above Carriden Beach. At the far end an (open) gate lead on to a gravel path to Shore Wood. This section, just over a mile long, keeps the woods mainly to your right, with the path sitting just in above the shore. You'll pass the remains of ***small building*** and the turning back up to Carriden Steadings. The surface is very rough here, and feels like going over an old drove road. It finally returns to (smooth) surfaced just as you exit the woods, when the view opens up across the Forth. Bar maybe 3-5m at its steepest drop (read vertical drop onto rocks) there is no safety fencing, and the path is just over a metre wide at best. Provided it's not wet or windy it is still an easy ride if you don't need to keep left for walkers or cyclists coming toward you. Otherwise slowing right down gives you more time to admire the view without risk of slipping off onto the part rocky beach. Of course, you can choose to take a break on the beach below. If so, consider carrying your bike down with you, as a courtesy to anyone else going down the narrow path.
	You will very soon see the Forth Bridges and Blackness Castle in the distance, with boats docked in the water in front of it. The field below Carriden Steadings on your right will sometimes have sheep or cattle grazing behind the fence. The larger cows may be a bit scary if right next to the fence, but it's higher on their end than appears from the path, they can't reach you.

*** the alternative Loony Dook - Fri, 2nd Jan

Only a day late (note Fri the 2nd!), and to usher in the new year, join us on a cycle out to the coast. We'll head out via the Innocent Railway and Newcraighall to the River Esk Path, before climbing up to the site of the Battle of Pinkie Cleugh. We'll continue through to Prestonpans and the 1722 Waggonway, to see the tables memorialising the flip in victors at the Battle of Prestonpans. Then it's on to Port Seton and through the greens at the caravan park to access the shared use path to Longniddry Dean. A very well hidden entrance to the Dean will take us downhill through the quiet, secluded woods, before emerging at Longniddry Bents 1, where we'll join the coastal path back to Edinburgh, via Morrison's Haven, the Ash Lagoons, Fisherrow Harbour and Brunstane Burns.

If the weather isn't being too kind we may shorten the ride and cut through to Levenhall Links at Prestonpans, where we'll explore both the old and the new bird hides along the Musselburgh Ash Lagoons.

Around 30 miles, and down to 20 if cut short, the ride is predominantly on off-road and segregated shared use paths and quiet back roads, with a couple short stretches with traffic Wallyford to Prestonpans, and on the on-road cycle path between Musselburgh and Joppa.



*** Dundas Estate & Lin Mill - Sat, 27th

It may not feel like it, but the days have started to grow longer - good excuse to go out and celebrate the extra 5min of daylight with a spin out to Dundas Loch and Castle. We'll head out via the CCWEL to the shared use path along the tram tracks, then turn off to enjoy a bit of quiet via SASA, before emerging back on to the path to the airport and Newbridge. We'll take the off-road track to Kirkliston and wend round the hidden paths before joining the B road and the shared use path toward South Queensferry. We'll enter the Dundas Estate at the gate to the Boathouse and cycle down for a view on the Loch from the south, before turning back and up to the castle. If lucky we may spot a llama or two, before cycling round the castle and on to the White Lodge, with panoramic views out to the bridges en route.
We'll then head toward the Forth via Lin Mill, before turning back to South Queensferry. We'll take the shared use path bordering the Dalmeny Estate and enter Edinburgh via Cramond Brig, and return to the Meadows via Barnton, the Blackhall and Roseburn Paths and the Roseburn to Union Canal link.

Around 25 miles, the ride is predominantly on off-road and segregated shared use paths and quiet back roads, with two very short sections with fast traffic to access the Dundas Estate from Kirkliston, and Lovers Lane from the access to the Forth Road Bridge.
